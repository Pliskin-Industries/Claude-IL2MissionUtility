# Flight 2F design: is it the server or the formation? The Yak raid in six formations at low load (2026-10-16)

Branch `claude/p14-flight-2f`, cut from `claude/p14-flight-2e` (`22bbde1`), fast-forwarded to `f185199` (2E's corrected spacing figures). Probe prompt: `handoff/P14-flight-2f-codex-prompt.md`.

## Why

Decision 0020 (Proposed) asks what the app should write for Pairs. In flight 2E (20 AI aircraft, mission time at about 0.85× wall time) the Yak airfield raid, as shipped (Pairs, Safe), collided in two of three runs; its Blue pair came within 9 m 12–13 s after `WP 1` in every run. Tacview shows each contact as an 80–90° attitude jump within 0.4 s.

The user's reading (2026-10-16): those jumps point to **erratic aircraft behaviour on a loaded server**, which could explain sudden collisions. So flight 2F asks one question first: **with the server well below its limit, do the collisions follow the formation, or do they happen in every formation?** The user's design: the Yaks only, Pairs at Dense, Safe and Loose, then Wedge at Dense, Safe and Loose.

Evidence so far, for the reading (not a ruling): flight 2D ran 16 aircraft at about 1.0× with no overload message, and its Dense Wedge still collided in two of three runs and its Dense Pairs in one. Flight 2F tests the Yak itself at a lighter load than any earlier flight.

## The idea: one Yak raid at a time, two at most

Every cell is the built-in `1950_DPRK_Yak_AirfieldRaid_2plus2.Group` (S4: two pairs sharing one `WP 1`, 4 Yak-9P, DPRK), parsed, placed by `place_air_sortie` and wrapped by the shell exactly as in flight 2E. The only change is to its two Formation commands, **after placement** (placement sets density 1 on every Formation command, so a source edit would be overwritten). Product code does not change.

- **Low load on purpose.** A Yak cell lives about 625 s from START to its `DELETE DELAY` (264 s transit, the shipped 300 s attack, 60.6 s to the delete). Starts 330 s apart mean **at most two cells (8 aircraft) fly at once**, half of flight 2D's 16 (1.0×) and well under 2E's 20 (0.85×). If the collisions were overload, they should mostly go away here; if they are the formation, Pairs should still close in and Safe Pairs should still come within metres.
- **Two targets, used in turn.** A is flight 2E's Yak target (C0c, ground 400–500 m); B is flight 2C's C2a (ground about 380 m), the most similar ground in the grid. Both paths lie on one north–south line (z = 0), 48.3 km apart end to end. A cell's target is reused only after the earlier cell there has been deleted (PD deleted at 685 s, PL starts at 720 s), so no two raids share the sky over one target.
- **Order** as the user gave it: Pairs Dense, Safe, Loose, then Wedge Dense, Safe, Loose.
- **One mission, not two.** All six fly under the same load, in one load per run.

### Cost

## What to measure (three runs, Tacview)

`tools/p14/flight2e_spacing.py` adapted to six cells (`flight2f_spacing.py`, written after the flight; cells by activation time, flights by Tacview `Name` suffix):

- **Server load, per run**: mission time against wall time (log file timestamps, as flights 2C to 2E), and any overload message (Esc shows the chat).
- **Attitude jumps away from other aircraft** (the server test): every 0.4 s step where an aircraft's roll, pitch or heading changes by 60° or more, with the nearest aircraft's distance at that moment. Contact predicts jumps only at a close pass; erratic server behaviour predicts jumps with no aircraft near.
- **Losses**, with attacker, height above ground and nearest aircraft 0.5 s before.
- **Same-flight spacing** per phase (transit, descent, on station), aircraft-seconds under 20 m, closest pass at Tacview's 0.2 s frames; other flight of the same cell.
- **The turn reversal** (2E: the lead reversed at about `WP 1` + 7 s, contact at + 12–13 s): per flight from `WP 1` to + 30 s, the wingman's slot before it (behind, right, below, in metres), whether the wingman crosses the lead's track, and the fore–aft and vertical separation at the crossing. Count later crossings on station too (2E run 3's Red pair collided at + 254 s).
- **No combat**: full ammunition at the end.

## Reading

- **Server.** With at most 8 aircraft at about 1.0×: if every formation shows the same close passes and attitude jumps with no aircraft near, the load (or another server effect) is the cause, not the formation. If the jumps come only at close passes, they are contacts.
- **Formation.** If the close passes follow the setting (Dense closest, Loose widest; Pairs closer than Wedge), the formation is the cause, and the cells compare the options of decision 0020 directly: PL for option A (Pairs at Loose), WS for option B or C (Wedge at Safe).
- **The control.** PS is 2E's shipped raid. If it comes within 20 m as in every 2E run, the light load did not remove the fault.
- Three runs are few; the close-pass counts and the crossing geometry are the stronger evidence, as in 2D and 2E.
